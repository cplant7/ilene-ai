Why AI value goes unmeasured, and how to fix it before launch.

Most AI initiatives are approved on expected benefits and reviewed on activity. The fix starts before launch: a baseline, a short list of measures and a named owner.

Measuring AI Value seriesPart 1 of 2October 20266 minute read

Twelve months after an AI initiative launches, someone asks a simple question: what did we get for it? Too often the answer is a list of activity. Licences bought, pilots launched, people trained. The value may be real, but nobody set up the measures to show it.

This two-part series sets out a practical way to measure AI value. Part 1 covers what needs to be in place before launch. Part 2 covers how to review, calculate and decide once the work is running.

Three reasons value goes unmeasured

1

No baseline

Performance before launch was never recorded, so any improvement afterwards is an estimate that is easy to dispute.

2

Activity, not outcomes

Logins, prompts and pilots show use, not value. They are useful signals, but they are not results.

3

No owner

Nobody is accountable for the number, so it is not reviewed and nothing changes when it slips.

None of these is a technology problem. Each is a management decision that was not made before the money was spent.

Measure the whole chain

AI value builds in four layers. Each depends on the one before it, so measure all four and read them from left to right.

Layer 1

Adoption

Is it being used as intended?

Active users as a share of the target group. Share of eligible work that goes through the tool.

Layer 2

Performance

Is the work better?

Cycle time, error and rework rate, first-contact resolution, throughput per person.

Layer 3

Outcome

Did the business result move?

Cost per transaction, backlog, customer satisfaction, revenue per account.

Layer 4

Value

What is it worth, net of cost?

Annual net benefit, payback period, return on investment.

The measurement chain. Example measures for each layer.

Reading the chain in order turns a disappointing result into a diagnosis:

Adoption is lowNothing downstream can be judged yet. Fix access, training and trust first.
Adoption is high, performance is flatThe tool is used, but the workflow around it has not changed. Revisit the process, not the licence count.
Performance improves, the outcome does not moveThe gain is absorbed elsewhere, for example time saved that is not redeployed. Decide where the capacity should go.
The outcome moves, net value is negativeRun costs exceed the benefit at current volume. Reconsider scope, pricing or scale.

Set the baseline before launch

Five decisions, made before go-live, separate an initiative that can show its value from one that can only describe it.

  1. 1
    Name the outcome and its ownerState the business result the initiative exists to move, and name the person accountable for it. If no one will own the number, the initiative is not ready.
  2. 2
    Choose a few measuresPick one or two measures for each layer of the chain. A short list gets reviewed; a long one gets ignored.
  3. 3
    Record todayMeasure current performance over a representative period, such as the last full quarter, using the same definitions you will use after launch.
  4. 4
    Agree the decision ruleDecide in advance what result means continue, adjust or stop, and by when. This keeps the later review about evidence, not advocacy.
  5. 5
    Fix the data sourceConfirm where each number comes from, who reports it and how often, before the first review is due.
Already launched without a baseline?

You can often recover one. Use system data from the period before launch, or compare against a team or region that has not adopted the tool yet. Record the method so the comparison can be repeated.

Choosing measures by function

The right KPIs depend on the workflow. The examples below are starting points to adapt, not targets. Choose measures you can track the same way before and after launch.

FunctionAdoption signalPerformance KPIBusiness outcome
Customer serviceShare of inquiries handled with AI assistanceAverage handling time; first-contact resolutionCost per contact; customer satisfaction
FinanceShare of invoices processed without manual keyingProcessing time per invoice; exception rateCost per invoice; days to close the month
Operations and supply chainShare of planners using forecasts in decisionsForecast accuracy; late schedule changesInventory carrying cost; on-time delivery
Human resourcesShare of policy questions answered through self-serviceTime to answer; time to fill rolesHR cost per employee; new-hire retention at 90 days
Sales and marketingShare of proposals drafted with AI supportProposal turnaround time; content production timeWin rate; cost per qualified lead
IT service deskShare of tickets triaged automaticallyMean time to resolve; reopen rateCost per ticket; user satisfaction
Public sector and programsShare of applications pre-screened with AI supportProcessing time per application; error rateBacklog size; service standard met
Legal and complianceShare of standard contracts reviewed with AI supportReview time per contract; issues found at final reviewExternal legal spend; contract cycle time

With the baseline set and the measures chosen, the initiative is ready to launch in a way that can show what it returns. The next step is to review it with discipline.
