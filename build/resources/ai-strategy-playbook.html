AI Strategy Playbook

Decide where AI can improve service, what to fund and when to expand.

For executives, service owners and the teams implementing AI. Each chapter ends with a decision or a usable piece of the strategy.

The running example

A resident needs a reliable answer.

Follow a municipal service team as it tests AI-assisted replies against its existing process. The same decisions apply to any customer support desk, public or private.

What this playbook gives you

  • Six phases, from Discover to Measure & Improve, each with a named owner and the evidence it needs.
  • A worked business case that shows when a pilot pays back and when it does not.
  • Pilot gates, acceptance tests and a 90-day calendar you can adapt.
  • An executive decision record to complete for every funding decision.

Version 1.0, November 2026. All figures are illustrative, in Canadian dollars, and are not benchmarks. Replace them with your own baseline before making a funding decision. This guide is not legal or financial advice.

01 · Discover · AI Strategy

Define the ambition

Agree on one customer outcome before choosing a product. A narrow first commitment gives the team room to learn at a known cost.

A strategy statement the sponsor can approveOver 90 days, test whether staff can answer routine resident questions faster while preserving accuracy. Staff approve every reply. Further funding is released only after the sponsor reviews the evidence.
Customer outcomeReduce the time a resident waits for a correct answer. Track repeat contacts within seven days so a fast but incomplete reply is visible.
Business outcomeRecover staff capacity for complex cases. Record how the manager will use the time, such as clearing a backlog during peak intake.
Scope and exclusionsStart with general hours, collection schedules and published service rules. Refer account-specific questions, eligibility decisions and appeals to a specialist.
Investment appetiteName the sponsor, the maximum pilot spend and the decision date. Include staff review time, integration, training and ongoing supervision in the estimate.
Decision to recordThe sponsor signs off the outcome, budget ceiling and exclusions. If the problem has no baseline, fund measurement first (page 13).

02 · Prioritize · AI Strategy

Choose the first investment

Have the service owner and frontline staff score the same shortlist independently, then discuss the assumptions behind the largest differences.

Opportunity scores

Illustrative weighted score out of 5. Inputs at right: value / feasibility / readiness.

Draft routine replies
4.55 / 4 / 4
Summarize case notes
3.54 / 3 / 3
Forecast staffing
3.13 / 4 / 2

Score each input from 1 to 5. Weight value at 50%, feasibility at 30% and readiness at 20%. Example: 5 × 0.5 + 4 × 0.3 + 4 × 0.2 = 4.5.

Scoring anchorsValue rises with measurable customer impact. Feasibility rises with tool fit and low integration effort. Readiness rises with usable data, a named owner and staff capacity.
Eligibility checkPark any option without approved data access or a credible human review path. Revisit it when the owner resolves the dependency.
Cheaper alternativeCompare reply drafting with refreshed templates and better policy search. Choose the lower-cost option if it achieves the same service outcome.

03 · Plan & Design · AI Strategy

Design the service

Map one real request from intake to resolution. Show who can see the information, who can act and where a person takes over.

A staff-reviewed reply

  1. A clerk removes personal identifiers and selects an approved question category.
  2. The assistant drafts a reply from current service policies and returns the source passage.
  3. The clerk checks the passage, corrects the draft and sends the reply. Missing or conflicting guidance goes to a specialist.
Choose the approachStart with approved templates. Test an existing platform's drafting feature if templates fall short. Fund a custom integration only when the pilot shows value and the platform cannot meet a documented need.
Control the dataThe policy owner approves sources and their expiry dates. IT verifies access, retention, deletion and how the supplier uses prompts. Test a prohibited input and an outdated policy before launch.
FallbackWhen a source is missing or the tool is unavailable, staff use the existing policy search and manual reply path. The clerk keeps ownership of the case.

Build the investment case

A pilot can save minutes and still cost more than the capacity it frees. Model the volume of eligible work and the share staff will actually use.

AssumptionsThree net minutes recovered per assisted case, including review, at $45 per staff hour, or $2.25 per case. At 50% adoption: 1,000 cases × 3 minutes ÷ 60 × $45 = $2,250 per month.
Break-evenAt $2.25 per assisted case, 800 cases (40% adoption) cover the monthly running cost. A $9,000 setup cost takes 20 months to recover at 50% adoption, assuming stable volume and costs.
Funding decisionAt 50% adoption the margin is $450 per month in capacity value. Before recording a financial benefit, Finance must identify usable hours, avoided overtime or an actual budget reduction.

04 · Implement · AI Strategy

Prove it in a pilot

Agree on release thresholds before customers see any change. The figures below are sample targets for the service desk example.

Test designCompare AI-assisted drafting with improved templates. Assign eligible cases to both groups during the same weeks. Balance request types and staff experience. Keep human approval in both groups.
Time and qualityRecord median handling time, including review. Have a second reviewer check replies against the policy in force without knowing which group produced them. Also track seven-day repeat contacts.
Illustrative gatesAt least 3 minutes saved per case on average (the cost model assumption), at least 98% policy accuracy, and repeat contacts no higher than the comparison group. Any critical disclosure pauses the pilot.
Evidence sufficiencyStart with 100 replies per group for a first read. Check variation and results for each request type. Extend the test if rare errors, a small subgroup or a close result could change the decision.
Decision at the pilot reviewScale one queue if the quality gates hold and measured time, volume and adoption support the cost model. Redesign if a correctable failure dominates. Stop if the simpler alternative delivers comparable value.

05 · Adopt · AI Strategy

Scale with accountability

Release to one additional team at a time. Assign a service owner with the authority to pause the tool and restore the manual process.

Daily: team leadReview flagged replies and failed source retrieval. Give staff one place to report a bad answer with its source and case reference. Route urgent incidents to the service owner immediately.
Weekly: service ownerSample sent replies, including low-volume categories. Review adoption, correction time and repeat contacts by queue. Coach staff on recurring errors and update the source policy where needed.
On change: IT leadVersion the tool, prompt and approved sources. Re-run a reference set of routine, ambiguous and prohibited requests after each change. Restore the prior version if quality drops.
When an answer causes harmPause the affected workflow. Preserve the relevant version and case record, identify affected recipients, assign corrections and test the fix. The service owner documents the restart decision.

06 · Measure & Improve · AI Strategy

Track value and improve

Compare what the service delivers with what the business case assumed. Use each review to decide where the next dollar goes.

Monthly: sponsorCompare actual costs and redeployed hours with the business case. Decide whether the next investment goes to a wider rollout, a bottleneck fix or another opportunity.
Three measuresAdoption: the share of eligible cases that staff assist with the tool. Value: the hours redeployed and what they were used for. Risk: policy accuracy, repeat contacts and incidents.
Count value onceReport capacity value and cash savings separately. Finance records a cash saving only when spend actually falls. The worked case on page 18 shows the numbers.
Improve the serviceFeed recurring errors back into the approved sources and staff coaching. Re-run the reference tests after each change, and compare the result with the pilot baseline.
Decision to recordAt each review the sponsor continues, changes or stops the funding and records the reason in the executive decision record (page 12).

The first 90 days

Use this calendar to reserve people and decision time across the six phases. Move ahead only when the named owner can show the evidence for the next commitment.

Days 1–15

Observe live work, sample past requests and establish current handling time, accuracy and repeat contacts.

Gate: the sponsor approves the mandate and pilot ceiling.

Days 16–30
Prioritize · Plan & Design

Select and design

Score the options, improve the template baseline and map the proposed service. Check approved sources, controls and costs.

Gate: the service owner authorizes a bounded test.

Days 31–60
Implement

Run the comparison

Train reviewers, assign cases and hold a weekly error review. Capture staff effort and follow up on repeat contacts.

Gate: the pilot lead submits results and material exceptions.

Days 61–90

Worked case 01 · Discover · AI Strategy

Establish a usable baseline

A resident writes twice about the same missed collection. The desk counts two closed tickets. The resident experienced one unresolved problem.

Define the unit of work

Use the original enquiry as the unit. Link any repeat contact within seven days to the original case and count the time spent on both. Allow the final week of cases seven more days to mature before comparing repeat rates.

MeasureWorking definition
EligibilityTag every incoming case by category before assignment. Exclude account changes, disputes and cases that require an identity check. Record the exclusion reason.
Active handling timeStart when a clerk opens the case. Include lookup, drafting, approval, correction and linked rework. Record queue wait separately.
Policy accuracyAn independent reviewer compares the sent answer with the policy in effect on the date of contact. Materially wrong or unsupported guidance fails.
Customer outcomeMeasure elapsed time from intake to the first correct answer. Segment by request type so a shift toward easy cases is visible.
Evidence to bring to PrioritizeThe analyst reconciles eligible plus excluded cases to total intake, reports missing timestamps and checks a sample against the original tickets. Flag unreliable measures before they are used in a funding decision.

Worked case 02 · Prioritize · AI Strategy

Choose the delivery approach

Start with the cheapest credible way to remove the delay. A better policy lookup may remove most of the work before a model drafts a single sentence.

OptionRun this testWhat would justify more spend?
A. Improve templatesGive clerks current policy links and three approved reply patterns. Time representative cases.A measurable delay remains in adapting replies or locating the correct exception.
B. Use an existing platformCompare its source-linked drafts with option A on the same cases. Include review, rejected drafts and rework.The incremental time saving covers the incremental cost, and answers meet the quality gates.
C. Build an integrationTrace the remaining manual handoff. Estimate volume, effort, failure handling and maintenance.A material platform limitation persists, and removing it is worth the added build and support cost.

Challenge the ranking

In the example on page 5, routine replies score 4.5. If the policy owner finds that key exceptions have no maintained source, suspend that candidate. A weighted score helps prioritize eligible work. It cannot authorize an unowned source.

A decision the sponsor can signFund a time-limited comparison of A and B. Defer custom integration. The service owner returns with the incremental minutes saved, the repeat-contact rate and the cost per assisted case.

Worked case 03 · Plan & Design · AI Strategy

Test the service before launch

Run these acceptance tests with a clerk, a policy owner and the person who can disable drafting. Record the policy version, actual response, reviewer and outcome.

Test caseExpected behaviourRelease evidence
Current ruleA routine collection enquiry cites the current approved schedule.The reviewer opens the citation and verifies the answer against the effective date.
Conflicting rulesA temporary holiday notice conflicts with the standard schedule.The draft cites the applicable exception or routes to a person. An unsupported choice fails.
Outside scopeA resident includes an account number and requests a billing adjustment.The clerk routes the case to the existing account process before any information reaches the drafting tool.
Source unavailableThe approved policy lookup is unavailable during a live shift.The clerk completes the case manually. The team lead can switch the queue back and reconcile pending cases.
Policy changesA new schedule takes effect after an earlier test passed.The owner retires the old version and reruns affected cases before the revised source is released.
Release ruleKeep the expected answer and actual result for each test. A failed boundary or fallback test keeps the affected category on the manual path. The service owner signs the retest and records the version released.

Worked case 04 · Plan & Design · AI Strategy

Stress-test the investment

A positive monthly margin can still miss the funding horizon. Test the setup cost and the share of recovered time the team can actually use.

Illustrative, CAD. 2,000 eligible cases per month at $45 per staff hour.

CaseAdoptionNet min. savedMonthly capacity valueAfter $1,800 running costSetup recovery*
Downside40%2.0$1,200−$600Not recovered
Base50%3.0$2,250$45020 months
Upside75%3.0$3,375$1,5755.7 months

* $9,000 setup cost divided by the positive monthly margin. Assumes steady volume and full use of recovered capacity from month one. A slower ramp-up delays recovery.

Set the hurdle before funding

To recover setup within 12 months, the service must cover the $1,800 running cost plus $750 of setup recovery each month. At $2.25 per assisted case, that requires 1,134 cases per month, or about 57% adoption. At 50% adoption, first-year capacity value after all costs is −$3,600.

Check what the hours are worthAt 50% adoption the service frees 50 hours a month. If only 30 hours can be redeployed, recognized capacity value is $1,350, a $450 monthly shortfall against running cost. Finance records a cash saving only when spend actually falls.

Worked case 05 · Implement · AI Strategy

Read the pilot results

Illustrative results from 100 eligible cases per group. Both groups receive staff review. The AI group includes assigned cases where the clerk rejected the draft.

MeasureTemplatesAI-assistedDecision signal
Mean active time12.0 min8.8 min3.2 minutes recovered per assigned case. Meets the 3-minute gate.
Policy-correct replies99 / 10098 / 100Meets the illustrative 98% threshold overall.
Repeat within 7 days5 / 1007 / 100Misses the gate of no increase.
Critical disclosures00None observed in this sample.

Find the failure hidden by the average

In the 20 holiday-exception cases in each group, there were 4 repeats with AI and 1 with templates. Across the other 80 cases, there were 3 repeats with AI and 4 with templates. Read all seven AI-group repeat cases before attributing the difference to the tool.

Keep the denominator consistent

The 3.2-minute result is per case assigned to AI, including cases where the clerk rejected the draft. Apply it to the expected number of assigned cases under the same usage conditions. Multiplying again by the draft acceptance rate would discount the saving twice.

Sponsor decision: revise and retestHold expansion. Route holiday exceptions to the existing process, investigate the repeat contacts and rerun the comparison after the source is fixed. The small subgroup shows where to investigate. It cannot establish the cause.

Worked case 06 · Measure & Improve · AI Strategy

Convert capacity into value

The service owner needs a plan for where the recovered hours go. A monthly total on a dashboard does not clear a backlog or change an overtime bill.

Benefit recordWorked example and evidence
Operational capacityAt base adoption, 1,000 assisted cases × 3 minutes = 50 hours per month. Validate against case and time records.
Committed useThe manager schedules 30 hours for backlog work. At 30 minutes per case, the planning estimate is 60 additional completions. Log actual completions and case mix.
Financial treatmentValue the 30 redeployed hours at $1,350 for capacity reporting. Count cash separately, using a verified change in payroll or contractor spend. Count each hour once.
Owner and reviewThe queue manager reviews use weekly. Finance reconciles costs monthly. The sponsor assesses the funding hurdle after the first full month at planned volume.

Before transferring to another queue

The receiving lead demonstrates the fallback, names the policy owner, validates local volume and signs the staffing plan. Recalculate the economics using that queue's case mix, review time and incremental costs.

Close the loopUse the executive decision record on page 12. Record the amount released, the categories still excluded, the person responsible for the next evidence and the review date. If redeployment falls short, revise the staffing plan or reduce the spend.
