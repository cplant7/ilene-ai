AI value measurement

Measuring AI Value

A practical guide to baselines, KPIs and review cadence, so the return on each AI initiative can be seen, tested and managed.

Most AI initiatives are approved on expected benefits and then reviewed on activity: licences bought, pilots launched, people trained. The value only becomes visible when measures are set before launch, tied to a business outcome and owned by a named person.

01

Why AI value goes unmeasured

1No baseline

Performance before launch was never recorded, so any improvement afterwards is an estimate that is easy to dispute.

2Activity, not outcomes

Logins, prompts and pilots show use, not value. They are useful signals, but they are not results.

3No owner

Nobody is accountable for the number, so it is not reviewed and nothing changes when it slips.

02

The measurement chain

Measure four layers. Each depends on the one before it.

Layer 1

Adoption

Is it being used as intended?

Active users as a share of the target group. Share of eligible work that goes through the tool.

Layer 2

Performance

Is the work better?

Cycle time, error and rework rate, first-contact resolution, throughput per person.

Layer 3

Outcome

Did the business result move?

Cost per transaction, backlog, customer satisfaction, revenue per account.

Layer 4

Value

What is it worth, net of cost?

Annual net benefit, payback period, return on investment.

Read the chain from left to right. Strong adoption with no change in performance is a signal to revisit the workflow, not to buy more licences.

03

Set the baseline before launch

1

Name the outcome

State the business result the initiative exists to move, and name the person accountable for it.

2

Choose few KPIs

Pick one or two measures for each layer of the chain. A short list gets reviewed; a long one gets ignored.

3

Record today

Measure current performance over a representative period, such as the last full quarter, using the same definitions you will use after launch.

4

Set the decision rule

Agree in advance what result means continue, adjust or stop, and by when.

5

Fix the data source

Confirm where each number comes from, who reports it and how often, before the first review is due.

05

Guardrail metrics: measure what could get worse

Headline KPIs can improve while quality, experience or fairness slips. Track a guardrail for each risk and set a threshold that triggers a review, even when the headline numbers look strong.

Quality

Error rate in a sample checked by a person; rework; corrections after release.

Experience

Complaints, escalations to a person, and customer and employee satisfaction.

Fairness and compliance

Outcome differences across groups where relevant; policy exceptions; audit findings.

Cost to run

Usage-based fees, support effort and vendor costs as volume grows.

06

Common measurement traps

!Counting time saved as money saved

Hours saved are a benefit only when they are redeployed to valued work or remove a cost. Record where the time went.

!Comparing against a different period

Seasonality, staffing and volume changes can look like AI impact. Compare like for like, or use a comparison group.

!Leaving out the full cost

Include licences, usage fees, integration, support, training and the time of the people involved, not only the vendor invoice.

!Changing the definition midway

If a KPI definition changes, restate the baseline. Otherwise the trend reflects the change in definition, not the work.

Reviewing and deciding

07

Review cadence: 30, 60 and 90 days

30daysAdoption
  • Is the target group using it as intended?
  • What is getting in the way of use?
  • Are guardrail metrics within threshold?
DecideRemove adoption barriers before judging results.
60daysPerformance
  • Are performance KPIs moving against the baseline?
  • Is quality holding in checked samples?
  • Where does the workflow still break?
DecideAdjust the workflow, training or configuration.
90daysValue
  • Has the business outcome moved?
  • What is the net value against the full cost?
  • What would it take to scale?
DecideScale, adjust or stop, using the rule agreed before launch.

After 90 days, review quarterly alongside your other operating metrics.

08

Calculating net value

Annual benefit(baseline cost per unit − new cost per unit) × annual volume + other measured benefits
Annual net valueannual benefit − annual run cost − build cost spread over its expected life

Count only benefits you can measure, or that the budget owner agrees are real. Revenue effects need the same baseline discipline as cost effects.
